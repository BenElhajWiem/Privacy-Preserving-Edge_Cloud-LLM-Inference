# Privacy labeling policy

You label private information in a user's request to an AI assistant. The request will be
processed by a small model on the user's device, which may forward parts of it to a cloud model.
Assume the cloud provider is honest but curious: it answers correctly, but it reads and keeps
everything it receives. Your labels decide what may leave the device.

## Labels

**public**: may leave the device unchanged. The text identifies nobody and reveals nothing personal
about the user or other people in the request. Examples: generic roles ("a manager", "a nurse"),
well-known public entities mentioned as a topic rather than as a link to the user ("Python",
"the French Revolution", "Tesla's stock price"), widely known places used generically ("tips for
visiting Paris").

**abstractable**: may leave only in a generalized form that keeps what the task needs and drops what
identifies. Typical quasi-identifiers:
- the user's employer, school or organization → "a mid-size tech company", "a UK university"
- a specific city or venue tied to the user → "a city in the UK", "a conference hotel"
- dates and times tied to the user → "next week", "in five days"
- ages → "in her 30s"
- amounts tied to the user → a range ("a salary around 50-60k")
- job titles that are rare enough to identify someone

**local-only**: never leaves, nor anything that would reveal it. This covers:
- names of private people (the user, colleagues, family, patients, clients), including first names
  and nicknames
- contact details: email addresses, phone numbers, street addresses, usernames, URLs of personal
  pages
- account, card, policy, case, passport, ID, license and other reference numbers
- sensitive attributes: health conditions, diagnoses, medication, sexual orientation, religion,
  political views, criminal records, immigration status, the exact finances of a person
- any detail that, even generalized, would still single out the person in context

## Rules

1. When in doubt between two labels, choose the stricter one (public < abstractable < local-only).
2. Context decides. The same text can be public in one request and local-only in another
   (a hospital named as a travel landmark versus as the place where the user is being treated).
3. Label each item for what it reveals in this request, not for how sensitive the word usually is.
4. For abstractable items, give a generalized form that keeps what the task needs.
5. Names of private people are always local-only, even when they appear only as a first name.

## Clarifications (v1.1, added after the model-based label review on 2026-10-21)

These cover the cases two independent annotators disagreed on most often. They refine the rules above;
they do not change the three labels.

6. **Fictional characters** in a story, game or script are public, unless the text says they stand for
   the user or for real people the user knows.
7. **Authors and people named in published material** that the user quotes or asks about (a cited paper,
   a byline, a news article, a press release, a court decision, a public profile) are public. Private
   individuals mentioned in passing inside such material (a client, a relative) stay local-only.
8. **Template placeholders and field descriptions** ("<Full name of candidate>", "[location not
   specified]") are public: they contain no information.
9. **Text written for publication.** Organizations, places, products and dates in content the user is
   preparing for or copying from public channels (an advert, a press release, a public tender, marketing
   copy, a published case) are public. The same entity is abstractable when the request ties it to the
   user privately (their employer, school, clinic, their own unpublished plans). A draft about the user's
   own work (their abstract, CV, cover letter, thesis) is not yet public: entities in it that point to the
   user, such as their institution or study site, stay abstractable. *(Added after the adjudication pass;
   see docs/data/pupa.md.)*
10. **Contact details.** Contact details that reach a specific person (a named email, a direct or mobile
    number, a home address, a personal handle) are local-only. Generic contact channels an organization
    publishes for everyone (a general hotline, a tenders@ address, a head-office address) are public.
11. **Identifying titles and links.** The title of the user's own thesis, paper or project, and links to
    their private resources (course pages behind a login, personal documents) single the user out:
    local-only. A link that only reveals an institution is abstractable.
